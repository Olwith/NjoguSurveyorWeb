How to Transfer Land Ownership in Kenya: A Complete Step-by-Step Guide

May 1, 2026 9 min read Njogu Surveyor Team
Back to Blog

Transferring land ownership in Kenya involves a structured legal process designed to protect both buyers and sellers. Whether you're buying land from a private seller, inheriting property, or receiving land as a gift, understanding the transfer process is essential to ensure a smooth and legally valid transaction.

This comprehensive guide walks you through every step of the land transfer process in Kenya, from initial due diligence to receiving your new title deed.

Types of Land Transfers in Kenya

Before diving into the process, it's important to understand the different types of land transfers recognized under Kenyan law:

  • Sale/Purchase: The most common type, where land is transferred from a seller to a buyer in exchange for payment.
  • Inheritance/Succession: Transfer of land to beneficiaries after the death of the owner.
  • Gift/Donation: Voluntary transfer of land without monetary consideration.
  • Exchange: Swapping one parcel of land for another.
  • Court Order: Transfer mandated by a court judgment.
Note

This guide focuses primarily on land transfers through sale/purchase, as it's the most common scenario. The process for other types may have slight variations.

Step-by-Step Process of Land Transfer in Kenya

Step 1: Conduct Due Diligence

Before any money changes hands, the buyer must verify the legitimacy of the property:

  • Conduct an official search at the Lands Registry to confirm ownership
  • Verify the title deed is genuine and not forged
  • Check for any encumbrances, charges, or caveats
  • Confirm the land boundaries match the title description
  • Verify the seller's identity against the registered owner
Critical Warning

Never skip the due diligence step. Many land fraud cases in Kenya result from buyers failing to verify ownership and encumbrances before making payments.

Step 2: Negotiate and Draft Sale Agreement

Once due diligence is complete, both parties negotiate terms and draft a sale agreement. This document should include:

  • Full details of buyer and seller
  • Title number and property description
  • Purchase price and payment terms
  • Timeline for completion
  • Responsibilities of each party
  • Dispute resolution mechanism

Step 3: Pay Deposit and Secure Financing

Typically, the buyer pays a 10% deposit upon signing the sale agreement. The remaining balance is usually paid before or during the transfer process. If financing through a bank, the buyer must secure mortgage approval at this stage.

Step 4: Obtain Land Control Board (LCB) Consent

For agricultural land, LCB consent is mandatory. The process involves:

  • Both parties appear before the LCB in the sub-county where the land is located
  • LCB verifies the transaction is voluntary and fair
  • LCB ensures the transaction doesn't disadvantage any party
  • Consent is granted, denied, or conditional
LCB Meetings

LCB meetings are typically held once a month. If you miss a sitting, you must wait for the next one. Plan your timeline accordingly.

Step 5: Prepare Transfer Documents

Your conveyancing lawyer prepares the transfer documents, including:

  • Transfer forms (Form LTA 1 for freehold, Form LTA 2 for leasehold)
  • Three copies of the sale agreement
  • Copies of ID/Passport for both parties
  • Passport photos of the buyer
  • KRA PIN certificates
  • Marriage certificate (if applicable)
  • Spousal consent (if married)
  • LCB consent form (for agricultural land)
  • Clearance certificates (rates, rent, land rent)

Step 6: Valuation and Stamp Duty

The Chief Government Valuer assesses the property to determine the market value for stamp duty purposes:

  • Urban land: 4% of the market value
  • Rural land: 2% of the market value

Stamp duty is paid via the iTax portal, and the receipt is attached to the transfer documents.

Step 7: Execute the Transfer

Both parties sign the transfer forms in the presence of a commissioner for oaths or advocate. The seller hands over the original title deed to the buyer's advocate.

Step 8: Lodgement at Lands Registry

The buyer's advocate lodges the complete transfer package at the relevant Lands Registry:

  • Original and duplicate transfer forms
  • Original title deed
  • Stamp duty receipt
  • Copies of IDs and KRA PINs
  • LCB consent (if applicable)
  • Rates clearance certificate
  • Valuation report

Step 9: Registration and Issuance of New Title

The Registrar of Titles reviews the documents, verifies everything is in order, and registers the transfer. The old title is cancelled, and a new title deed is issued in the buyer's name.

Timeline

The entire process typically takes 30 to 90 days, depending on the county, completeness of documents, and registry workload. Ardhisasa digital transactions are usually faster than manual filings.

Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

Watch Out For
  • Incomplete documentation: Missing documents cause significant delays. Use a checklist.
  • Unpaid rates/rent: Outstanding county rates or land rent must be cleared before transfer.
  • Spousal consent missing: Married sellers must obtain spousal consent, even if the land is registered in one name.
  • Fake LCB consent: Verify LCB consent directly with the board; forged consent is a common fraud tactic.
  • Under-declaration of value: Undervaluing property to reduce stamp duty is illegal and can result in penalties.

Using Ardhisasa for Digital Transfers

The Ardhisasa platform has digitized many land transactions in Nairobi and other counties. Benefits include:

  • Faster processing times
  • Reduced risk of document loss
  • Transparent tracking of application status
  • Digital payment of fees

However, Ardhisasa is not yet available in all counties. Check with your local Lands Registry to confirm if digital transfers are supported.
